# Answers

## Answer 1
Author: Bill Croft
Accepted: yes
Score: 1
License: CC BY-SA 3.0

That is not the number of active connections you have but rather the number of connect atempts. see this answer (https://stackoverflow.com/questions/7432241/mysql-show-status-active-or-total-connections).

There are a few ways to deal with this. The first and most obvious to increase the max connections - configure your server to deal with the growing demand whatever this means in your environment (ulimits, memory, CPU, my.cnf, etc..).

Another way to deal with this is to find out what connections the jobs and applications demand and see if you can reduce the number of concurrent jobs running simultaneously as the applications are in use.

Another solution is to discuss this with the developers. Ask them if the applications or jobs are creating any unnecessary connections or are holding connections open longer than necessary.

It's not uncommon to see a 5 minute job open a DB connection, check some information and then leave that DB connection open until the job ends just so it doesn't need to reconnect before it writes status info back to a queue.

## Answer 2
Author: Rick James
Accepted: no

4 apps times 200 in the pool? That's 800, which is more than 500.

But... Do you really every need 200? I doubt it. Look at the clients. Make sure they run fast and disconnect when finished. Then set the pool to a lower number. A much lower number, say 20.

Also look at `wait_timeout`. You can lower it to kick of clients that are sitting there doing nothing (such as the pools).

2568 Connections? Divide that by `Uptime` to get connections/second. What do you get? A value of less than 10 is pretty typical. More than 100 is probably stressing the system.

The processlist show very few threads connected, but clearly (`Max_used_connections = 501`) it did hit the roof at some time.

If you have more than, say, a dozen connections running, not just connected and `Sleeping`, then you need to look at the queries to see what can be improved.
